# Dove Golf product roadmap

The revival is organized around one user journey:

> Observe the shot → understand the evidence → test one change → check equipment only when the pattern persists.

The homepage should stay minimal. Depth belongs inside tools, explanations, and focused content—not in a crowded landing page.

## Phase 1: Earn trust with the core journey

### Landing page

- Match the established clean About-page language.
- Present one promise: read the shot and test the cause.
- Keep two primary entry points: Ball Flight Decoder and Equipment Fit Check.
- Explain that results separate evidence, uncertainty, and the next test.
- Do not put the full tool library, article catalog, or community feed on the homepage.

### Ball Flight Decoder

- Collect start direction, curve, and strike location.
- Explain face-to-target and face-to-path relationships in plain language.
- Clearly distinguish a likely relationship from something the inputs cannot prove.
- Give one focused range test before suggesting an equipment change.
- Link persistent or centered-strike problems into the Equipment Fit Check.

### Equipment Fit Check

- Preserve brand-neutral recommendations and explainable scoring.
- Frame equipment as one possible contributor, not an automatic cure.
- Use the golfer's observed pattern and existing inputs to reduce repeated questions when the two tools connect.

## Phase 2: Make the physics easy to see

Add restrained point-of-view explanations inside the diagnosis result rather than decorative homepage graphics.

Priority visual modules:

1. Clubface relative to target at impact.
2. Club path relative to target.
3. Face-to-path relationship and expected curve direction.
4. Heel, center, and toe contact with gear-effect caveats.
5. A comparison view showing the observed pattern and the single variable being tested.

Visuals should use simple lines, arcs, labels, and controlled motion. Every animation needs a static, text-equivalent explanation and reduced-motion behavior.

The explanatory rule is:

- **Observed:** what the golfer reported.
- **Relationship:** what ball-flight laws support.
- **Possible cause:** what remains a hypothesis.
- **Test:** what to change and compare next.

Dove Golf should not claim to identify a body-mechanics fault from ball flight alone.

## Phase 3: Build the free utility cluster

The next tools should each answer a narrow question and produce a testable output.

### Recommended order

1. **Launch & Spin Window Checker**  
   Uses club type, speed, launch, and spin to show whether measured numbers sit inside a reasonable starting window.

2. **Bag Gapping Checker**  
   Maps carry distances, highlights overlaps and large gaps, and suggests which distance to verify next.

3. **Strike Pattern Analyzer**  
   Uses repeated face-contact observations to distinguish random contact from a stable heel, toe, high, or low pattern.

4. **Distance & Dispersion Planner**  
   Helps beginners choose a safer target using carry range and left/right dispersion rather than a single perfect-shot number.

These tools can live on a dedicated `/tools` page. Only the most important one or two should appear on the homepage.

## Phase 4: Publish content that supports a real task

Content should answer questions that lead naturally into a tool or test.

Useful clusters include:

- Why the ball starts left or right.
- Why a slice can have more than one contributor.
- How strike location changes curve and speed.
- When launch and spin indicate technique, delivery, or equipment questions.
- How to test shaft weight, length, loft, and lie without buying first.
- Beginner range protocols that change one variable at a time.

Each article should include an original explanation, a relevant tool, a small test protocol, and a clear statement of limitations. Avoid generic news summaries and high-volume filler.

## Phase 5: Pilot community carefully

Community can become valuable after the tools generate repeat visits and a shared language.

Start with moderated golfer questions rather than a fully open anonymous board:

- Pseudonymous profiles or verified email.
- Optional region tags, kept broad enough to protect privacy.
- Structured question fields for ball flight, strike, equipment, and what was already tested.
- Owner or moderator approval for first-time posts.
- Reporting, rate limits, spam filtering, and clear conduct rules.
- No precise location sharing by default.

A later regional layer could support course discussions, practice partners, local fitting experiences, and events. It should be added only after moderation demand and privacy risks are understood.

## Measurement

Track the user journey rather than raw page views alone:

- Homepage to tool start.
- Tool completion rate.
- Result to recommended next-test interaction.
- Ball Flight Decoder to Equipment Fit Check transition.
- Return visits after a range test.
- Guide-to-tool and search-to-tool conversion.
- Saved, shared, or revisited results.
- Community questions that receive a useful response.

The initial traffic goal is more credible when the site creates repeatable utility, searchable explanations, and reasons to return.
